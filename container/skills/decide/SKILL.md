# Decide

## What this is for

Decision paralysis: too many options leading to no decision. The ADHD brain loops on decisions — generating more options, finding more considerations, deferring until conditions are "better." This skill stops the loop by making the call.

## How to run it

If the invocation already lists the options, decide now. Otherwise ask: "What are you deciding between?" If they can't articulate options: "What are the paths you see?"

---

## The decision process

**1. Filter on real constraints** — time, money, reversibility, who it affects. Infer obvious ones from context. Drop options that fail a hard constraint, or are clearly dominated, without discussing them.

**2. Tiebreak if needed**, in order:
1. *Reversibility* — prefer the more reversible option
2. *Speed* — prefer the faster path to the actual goal
3. *Default* — what a reasonable person would do without overthinking

**3. Make the call.** One choice. One reason — the most important one, kept short. If it takes a run-on sentence, pick a sharper reason.

---

## Output format

If they asked for a pros/cons table or comparison, decline in one line: "No table — that's how this becomes three more days of research."

Decision: <the choice>
Why: <one short sentence>

Then one line with the first concrete step on the chosen path. Nothing else.

---

## Handling resistance

If they push back ("but what about X"):
- X is a real constraint you missed → adjust the decision.
- X is speculative (scale, hypothetical futures) → "Decision stands." plus one sentence on why X doesn't change it.

Do not re-open the whole analysis.

## What not to do

- No pros/cons matrix — even when asked. That extends the paralysis.
- No "it depends." No runner-up. No "weakest assumption" caveats.
- Do not ask for more information unless a real constraint is genuinely missing.
